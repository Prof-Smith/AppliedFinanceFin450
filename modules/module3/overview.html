Module Purpose

From valuation engine to company diagnosis

Module 2 built the pro forma DCF valuation engine. Module 3 shifts the emphasis from valuation mechanics to company analysis: What drives the firm? Which operating relationships are stable? Where is value created? What risks could reverse the thesis?

Text connection: Chapter 5 illustrates company analysis by building a pro forma model for Merck. This module applies the same architecture to AbbVie so students can transfer the modeling process to a new company.
Responsible-use note: Default values and prompts are teaching scaffolds. Students should verify company data using AbbVie filings, investor materials, and current market data if required by the instructor.
Why AbbVie?

A similar industry, a different strategic story

AbbVie keeps Module 3 close to the Merck pharmaceutical example while giving students a different focal firm. AbbVie creates a useful case for analyzing product transition, portfolio concentration, operating margins, reinvestment, debt, cash flow durability, ROIC, and valuation sensitivity.

Continuity with the text
Like Merck, AbbVie is a global health care and biopharma company, so students can reuse the Chapter 5 modeling categories: sales projections, operating costs, balance sheet rewriting, debt, tax, FCF, ROIC, DCF, and multiples.
New analytical angle
AbbVie offers a clear product-transition discussion around legacy revenue, newer growth platforms, immunology, neuroscience, oncology, aesthetics, and pipeline or reinvestment risk.

Student Deliverable

AbbVie Company Analysis Memo

The final Module 3 artifact should be a concise professional memo, not just a set of model outputs.

Required sections

  • Business model and strategic issue
  • Historical financial statement analysis
  • Economic balance sheet and invested capital
  • Sales and product transition analysis
  • Operating cost and margin assumptions
  • Asset intensity and reinvestment needs
  • ROIC and value creation
  • Valuation cross-check
  • Risk, reversal condition, and responsible stewardship

Evidence carried forward

  • Revenue growth and CAGR evidence
  • Current assets and current liabilities as sales relationships
  • Operating cost driver assumptions
  • Net PPE and intangible asset intensity
  • Debt, cash, and net financial debt
  • Tax and cost of debt assumptions
  • FCF and ROIC estimates
  • DCF and peer multiple range

What makes a financial model useful?

A useful model organizes uncertainty, clarifies assumptions, and supports a decision. In Module 1, students build the mental structure that supports every later finance module.

1

Understand value through time

Students begin with time value of money, present value, and future value. The purpose is to understand why timing and required return shape every later valuation decision.

2

Evaluate cash-flow decisions

Students then use NPV and IRR to interpret project value, return thresholds, and the limitations of mechanical decision rules.

3

Read financial evidence

Students move from project math to company evidence through statements and ratios. The goal is to diagnose profitability, liquidity, leverage, efficiency, and cash-flow quality.

4

Estimate and compare value

Students build intrinsic value through DCF and then compare market value through enterprise value and valuation multiples.

5

Defend a recommendation

Students finish by writing a professional memo that includes a claim, numerical evidence, uncertainty, risk, and a reversal condition.

Module Evidence Map

What each section contributes to the final memo

Valuation mechanics

TVM, PV, FV, NPV, and IRR provide the logic for discounting and cash-flow decisions.

Company diagnosis

Financial statements and ratios provide context about financial quality and sustainability.

Intrinsic and market valuation

DCF estimates intrinsic value, while enterprise value connects the analysis to market pricing.

Professional judgment

The case challenge and AI reflection test whether students can communicate a defensible decision.

For each tool page, students should carry forward one claim, one number, and one uncertainty into the final analyst memo.

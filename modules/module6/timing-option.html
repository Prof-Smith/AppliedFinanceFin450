Timing Option: the value of waiting before committing capital

A timing option exists when management can delay investment while uncertainty may resolve. Waiting can preserve capital and improve decision quality, but waiting can also cost money through lost cash flows, competitive entry, or delayed strategic positioning.

Analyst purpose: Students should decide whether waiting adds value or destroys value, and explain what uncertainty is expected to resolve during the waiting period.
How the calculation works

Waiting as a call option with a delay cost

The option to wait can be viewed as a call option on the project. The underlying value is the present value of the project if undertaken later, and the exercise price is the required investment. Waiting has value because management can choose not to invest if future conditions are unfavorable. However, waiting may also impose a cost, such as foregone cash flows or strategic delay.

Underlying value, S = PV of project cash flows if investment is made later Exercise price, X = Investment required later Timing option ≈ Call option value Net Timing Value = Timing Option Value - Delay Cost Timing-Adjusted Value = Static NPV + Net Timing Value

The wait decision is strongest when uncertainty is meaningful, the investment can truly be delayed, and the delay cost is not too large. If competitors can capture the opportunity while the firm waits, the timing option may be worth less than the model suggests.

Student checkpoint: Identify what uncertainty is likely to resolve during the waiting period. If waiting does not produce better information, it may simply postpone value creation.

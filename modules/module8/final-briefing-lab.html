Final Briefing Lab: turn analysis into a briefing-ready recommendation

The final briefing should not be a dump of all prior calculations. It should synthesize valuation logic, strategic rationale, risk evidence, market reaction, and a clear recommendation that can be delivered professionally.

Analyst purpose: Students should convert the course evidence into a concise final briefing with an evidence hierarchy, recommendation, key risks, and reversal condition.
How the briefing works

From analysis archive to executive briefing

The final briefing is a communication product. It should prioritize the most decision-relevant evidence and explain how the market reaction affects the recommendation. Students should use market reaction evidence as one input, not as a substitute for valuation or strategic analysis.

Briefing Thesis = Recommendation + Main Evidence + Key Risk + Reversal Condition Evidence Hierarchy = Valuation Evidence + Strategic Evidence + Risk Evidence + Market Reaction Evidence Briefing Tone = Supportive, Qualified, or Cautious based on evidence strength

The briefing should be clear enough for an investment committee to understand the recommendation without seeing every intermediate calculation. The best briefings are concise, balanced, and explicit about uncertainty.

Student checkpoint: What is the strongest evidence, and what evidence would change the recommendation?

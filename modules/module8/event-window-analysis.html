Event Window Analysis: leakage, immediate reaction, delayed reaction, or noise?

The event window determines what market reaction evidence is counted. A narrow window focuses on the immediate event-day response. A wider window can capture anticipation, leakage, delayed processing, or continued information arrival, but it can also add noise.

Analyst purpose: Students should justify the event window they use and explain whether the evidence supports immediate reaction, leakage, delayed reaction, or weak market response.
How the calculation works

Selecting the window changes the story

The same daily abnormal returns can produce different conclusions depending on the window used. A one-day window focuses on Day 0. A three-day window usually captures the day before, the event day, and the day after. A five-day window adds a wider context for pre-event and post-event movement.

One-Day CAR = AR_0 Three-Day CAR = AR_-1 + AR_0 + AR_+1 Five-Day CAR = AR_-2 + AR_-1 + AR_0 + AR_+1 + AR_+2 Leakage Ratio = Pre-Event AR / Five-Day CAR Delayed Ratio = Post-Event AR / Five-Day CAR

A good event-study interpretation does not simply choose the largest CAR. It asks whether the selected window is appropriate for the information environment, the announcement timing, and the possibility of confounding news.

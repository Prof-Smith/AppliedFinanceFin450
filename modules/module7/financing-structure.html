Sprint 8CDeal financing

Financing Structure: how should the acquirer pay?

A transaction can look attractive on value creation but still become risky if the financing mix is poorly designed. This lab evaluates how cash, debt, and stock financing affect leverage, ownership dilution, financing cost, and the credibility of the overall deal recommendation.

Analyst purpose: Students should explain how the proposed financing mix changes transaction risk, not simply whether the deal can be funded.
How the calculation works

From purchase price to financing risk

The financing structure determines how the acquirer funds the purchase price. Cash reduces liquidity, debt increases leverage and interest burden, and stock dilutes existing shareholders. A balanced recommendation should identify the tradeoff between certainty of funding, balance sheet risk, and shareholder dilution.

Cash Funding = Purchase Price × Cash % Debt Funding = Purchase Price × Debt % Stock Funding = Purchase Price × Stock % New Debt / EBITDA = (Existing Debt + New Debt) / Combined EBITDA Interest Coverage = Combined EBIT / Interest Expense New Shares Issued = Stock Funding / Acquirer Share Price Pro Forma Ownership Dilution = New Shares Issued / (Existing Shares + New Shares Issued)

The financing mix is not good or bad in isolation. It must be judged against the target’s cash flow stability, synergy credibility, debt capacity, interest coverage, and the acquirer’s willingness to dilute existing shareholders.

Student checkpoint: Which financing source creates the biggest risk: liquidity pressure, leverage, or ownership dilution?

Debt Capacity: can the post-deal company support the financing?

A transaction can be strategically attractive, synergy-supported, and even accretive, but still become fragile if the post-deal company carries too much debt. This lab evaluates debt capacity using leverage, interest coverage, free cash flow coverage, and downside EBITDA cases.

Analyst purpose: Students should determine whether the balance sheet can support the transaction in both base and downside scenarios.
How the calculation works

From funded debt to credit capacity

Debt capacity is not simply how much debt can be borrowed at closing. The more important question is whether the combined business can service debt after the deal, especially if EBITDA falls short or synergies are delayed. This page compares actual leverage with a target leverage ceiling and tests whether interest and free cash flow coverage remain adequate under stress.

Pro Forma Debt = Existing Debt + New Debt - Cash Paydown Net Debt / EBITDA = Pro Forma Debt / EBITDA Interest Expense = Pro Forma Debt × Average Interest Rate Interest Coverage = EBIT / Interest Expense FCF After Interest = EBITDA - Cash Taxes - Capex - Interest Expense Debt Capacity at Target Leverage = Target Debt / EBITDA × EBITDA Debt Headroom = Debt Capacity - Pro Forma Debt

The downside case is especially important. If the deal only works when EBITDA and synergy assumptions are strong, the recommendation should clearly disclose that the financing plan is fragile.
